The guest kernel fixture compiles the production miniport.cpp with DriverEntry
renamed only to rtl8125_production_entry, and links the production resources,
chip and OID objects against real NDIS/HAL/kernel imports. Its own DriverEntry
calls the real production entry and checks the actual installed unload handler.
Initialization rejection cases call production callbacks directly. Adapter/Halt
tests construct labelled empty state using actual NdisAllocateMemoryWithTag;
they do not represent a successfully initialized hardware adapter.

ReactOS IopInitializeDriverModule clears DRVO_LEGACY_DRIVER for a driver which
creates no device objects. IopUnloadDriver then rejects SCM stop for such a PnP
driver with the misleading combined-condition message "No DriverUnload" even
when an unload callback exists. The guest-only harness creates an anonymous
control device, then its unload scaffold deletes that device and invokes the
actual production rtl8125_unload. This permits two isolated SCM start/stop cycles
and proves wrapper retirement. The production driver creates no such device and
its PnP unload behavior is unchanged. Initial failure transcripts are retained;
no claim of operational PnP NIC removal or networking follows from this fixture.

The runner copies only the guest fixture to its unique RTL8125M01Guest service,
reports through OutputDebugString and a guest text file, performs two start/stop
cycles and deletes the fixture service. COM2 is supplementary and may remain
empty if that serial port is not active in the snapshot. The authoritative
kernel serial log includes the runner's OutputDebugString reports.
